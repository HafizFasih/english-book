# Chapter 4 — Paragraph Writing

A **paragraph** is a collection of sentences that all relate to a single topic. Every good paragraph is focused, well-organised, and internally connected through transition words.

---

## 4.1 Structure of a Paragraph

Every paragraph has three essential parts:

| Part | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| **Topic Sentence** | States the main idea of the paragraph |
| **Supporting Sentences** | Provide evidence, details, and examples |
| **Concluding Sentence** | Summarises the topic or wraps up the argument |

![Paragraph structure diagram](/img/04-paragraph-writing/Paragraph%20writing%20and%20precis%20Writing_img001.png)

### The Topic Sentence

The topic sentence introduces the main idea of the paragraph without over-explaining. Everything extra — the evidence and analysis — belongs in the supporting sentences.

:::tip
Unless you are writing a narrative, your topic sentence should always be the first sentence of the paragraph.
:::

### Supporting Sentences

Supporting sentences develop the topic with:
- **Facts and data**
- **Examples and illustrations**
- **Explanation and analysis**
- **Quotations**

### The Concluding Sentence

The concluding sentence either summarises the paragraph's main point or provides a final piece of support that ties everything together.

![Paragraph structure example](/img/04-paragraph-writing/Paragraph%20writing%20and%20precis%20Writing_img002.png)

---

## 4.2 Characteristics of an Effective Paragraph

A strong paragraph:

1. **Explores a single topic** — every sentence relates to the main idea
2. **Uses logical order** — details follow in a sensible sequence
3. **Uses transitions** — connecting words link sentences smoothly
4. **Is appropriately developed** — not too short (underdeveloped) or too long (rambling)

![Effective paragraph characteristics](/img/04-paragraph-writing/Paragraph%20writing%20and%20precis%20Writing_img003.jpeg)

---

## 4.3 Transitions Within a Paragraph

Transition words and phrases guide the reader from one idea to the next without confusion.

| Function | Transition Words |
|----------|-----------------|
| **Sequence / order** | first, second, third, then, next, finally, subsequently |
| **Addition** | furthermore, moreover, in addition, also, besides |
| **Contrast** | however, on the other hand, although, yet, nevertheless |
| **Cause & effect** | therefore, as a result, consequently, hence, thus |
| **Example** | for example, for instance, such as, namely |
| **Emphasis** | indeed, in fact, above all, certainly |
| **Conclusion** | in summary, in conclusion, to sum up, overall |

## 4.4 Sample Paragraphs

### Sample 1 — Without Connectors
*Miami is the perfect place to take a vacation. It is always sunny and warm. The beaches are gorgeous, with soft white sand and beautiful water. There are many fine restaurants in the Miami area, and most of the hotels offer terrific entertainment nightly. It's no wonder that Miami is my first choice for a vacation destination.*

### Sample 2 — With Connectors
*Miami is the perfect place to take a vacation. **First**, it is always sunny and warm. **In addition**, the beaches are gorgeous, with soft white sand and beautiful water. **Furthermore**, there are many fine restaurants in the Miami area, and most hotels offer terrific entertainment nightly. **For these reasons**, Miami is my first choice for a vacation destination.*
