# Chapter 9 — Report Writing

**Reports** are the tools you use to analyse complex problems, educate audiences, address opportunities, win contracts, and launch new ventures. Effective report writing is a critical skill in every engineering and business profession.

---

## 9.1 What Is a Report?

A report provides important information for a specific audience, serves a defined purpose, and presents findings in a structured format. In the digital age, efficient reporting helps organisations sift through data and make major decisions — whether launching a product, expanding into new markets, reducing costs, or improving services.

### Formal vs. Informal Reports

| Type | Length | Examples |
|------|--------|---------|
| **Formal Report** | 20–200 pages | Research reports, annual reports, feasibility studies |
| **Informal Report** | 1–5 pages | Progress reports, incident reports, status updates |

---

## 9.2 Types of Reports

### Informational Reports
Provide **data or information without analysis or recommendations**.

Examples:
- Monthly sales reports
- Progress reports
- Trip reports
- Inventory status reports

### Analytical Reports
Provide **data along with analysis and recommendations**.

Examples:
- Feasibility reports
- Justification / recommendation reports
- Financial forecasts
- Research and investigation reports

---

## 9.3 Report Writing in the Digital Age

Business reports vary widely in length, purpose, and delivery:

- **Short reports** may be brief bulleted status updates
- **Formal reports** may span 100 pages with financial forecasts
- Reports may be delivered as emails, PDF files, slide decks, or intranet posts
- Many organisations store reports on cloud platforms for team access

---

## 9.4 Structure of a Formal Report

A formal report typically includes these sections:

| Section | Content |
|---------|---------|
| **Title Page** | Title, author, date, organisation |
| **Executive Summary / Abstract** | Brief overview of the entire report |
| **Table of Contents** | Page-by-page listing of sections |
| **Introduction** | Background, purpose, scope, and limitations |
| **Methodology** | How data was collected or the problem was approached |
| **Findings / Results** | The data and information gathered |
| **Discussion / Analysis** | Interpretation and implications of findings |
| **Conclusions** | Summary of what was learned |
| **Recommendations** | Suggested courses of action |
| **References / Bibliography** | All sources cited |
| **Appendices** | Supplementary material (charts, raw data, etc.) |

## 9.5 Report Formats

Reports can be delivered using any of the following formats:

- **Letter** — formal external communication
- **Memorandum / Email** — internal or digital communication
- **Forms & Templates** — standardised documents used repeatedly
- **Digital Formats** — PDFs, presentations, online dashboards
- **Manuscript** — traditional formal printed documents

## 9.6 Writing Style for Reports

### Language Principles
- **Objective** — avoid personal opinions unless in the recommendations section
- **Precise** — use exact figures and specific language
- **Concise** — say what needs to be said without padding
- **Clear** — use plain, professional English
- **Formal** — avoid contractions and slang

### Common Report Phrases

| Situation | Phrase |
|-----------|--------|
| Introducing findings | *"The data indicates that…"* |
| Making a recommendation | *"It is recommended that…"* |
| Citing a source | *"According to [Source],…"* |
| Drawing a conclusion | *"Based on the above findings,…"* |
| Summarising | *"In summary, the investigation revealed…"* |

## 9.8 Sub-types of Reports

### Trip / Convention / Conference Reports
Written by employees on business trips to document the events they attended and what they learned. These reports often inform management about business trends, procedures, legal requirements, or other information affecting their operations and products.

### Progress Reports
Written to update readers about the progress on an ongoing project.

### Minutes of Meetings and Summaries
**Minutes** summarise the proceedings of meetings.
A **summary** compresses the main points from a book, report, article, website, meeting, or convention — saving time by reducing a report or article by 85 to 95 percent.

### Justification / Recommendation Reports
Recommend an action to solve a problem, fulfil a need, or bring an improvement in a system.

## Practice Tasks

### Task 1 — Conference Report

Your class recently attended a conference related to your field, organised by your department. Write a detailed report for your class advisor covering:
- Introduction
- Purpose of attending the conference
- Conference arrangements
- Conference speakers
- Key learning areas
- Conclusion (and recommendations, if any)

### Task 2 — TECH FEST Report

TECH FEST 2023 was held at your department recently. The Head of Department (HOD) has asked you to prepare a **short report** mentioning the proceedings of the event. Include:
- Number of guests / speakers
- Number of participants
- Theme / topic of the event
- Summary of the speaker's talk

Use the **Block Letter Format**.

---

### Task 3 — Short Informational Report

Write a **short informational report** (1–2 pages) on one of the following topics:

1. The state of internet connectivity in your university campus
2. Waste management practices observed in your department building
3. Attendance patterns in your class over the past month

Your report should include:
- A title page
- An introduction (purpose and scope)
- Findings (what you observed)
- Conclusion
- At least one recommendation
