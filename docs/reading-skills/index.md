# Chapter 1 — Reading Skills

Reading comprehension is the ability to read a text, process it, and understand its meaning. Strong readers do not just decode words — they actively engage with the text by identifying main ideas, making inferences, and evaluating arguments.

---

## Reading Passage: Climate Change and Health

Read the passage carefully. Pay attention to the bold vocabulary words — you will be asked about them in the exercises below.

---

a. The devastating floods in Pakistan are a manifestation of climate change. Whatever development gains had been made over the decades in the flood-affected districts were washed away within a few days. Pakistan will take years to recover from this deluge. The nature of various manifestations of climate change — in the form of frequent extreme weather events, heavy and prolonged rains, cloudbursts, flash floods, heatwaves, wildfires, catastrophic storms, tsunamis, cyclones, droughts — make the future highly unpredictable.

b. A lot has been written about the causes of climate change and how those who pollute the least are affected the most. For almost three decades, the United Nations has been bringing together member states for global climate change summits. In 2015, the historic **Paris Agreement** was adopted by 196 world leaders with the aim of containing the increase in the average global temperature to below two degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels.

c. The 27th Conference of Parties (COP27) took place in Sharm El Sheikh, Egypt. In the words of the president-designate, *"the climate crisis is existential, overriding and ever present; adverse climate impacts are increasing in frequency, intensity and impacts."*

d. Climate change affects human health in many direct and indirect ways. Rising temperatures increase the frequency and intensity of heatwaves. Prolonged exposure to high temperatures produces cumulative physiological stress on the human body which **exacerbates** the top causes of distress and death globally, including respiratory and cardiovascular diseases, diabetes mellitus and renal disease.

e. Rising temperatures are changing ecosystems, favouring the survival of insects and pathogens. Mosquitoes and ticks thrive in warmer environments. Climate change pushes animals to new habitats, leading to new interactions between animals and humans — giving rise to the spread of **zoonotic** diseases such as rabies. Ebola, Lassa fever, Rift Valley fever and monkeypox are known to spread more widely under rising temperatures.

f. Apart from heat, there are direct effects of climate change depending on its manifestation at a given time and place — injuries, waterborne diseases, **exacerbation** of existing **chronic** conditions, mental health issues, and special needs of vulnerable groups such as women, young children, old people, and the disabled.

g. Pakistan is passing through a situation that the WHO has characterised as a *"public health crisis."* Climate change effects such as rising sea levels, **collapsing** ice shelves, and greater volcanic activity are linked to the increased threat of tsunamis. The 2004 tsunami alone caused 227,000 deaths and **unfathomable** misery.

h. The indirect and **insidious** health effects of climate change work by negatively affecting the **determinants** of health — social, economic, political, environmental, and behavioural factors that influence human health.

i. From a policy perspective, these factors are not typically in the control of health ministries and hence necessitate inter-sectoral collaboration.

j. Take education as an example. Research has concluded that approximately 14% and 30% decline in under-five and adult **mortality** between 1970 and 2010 resulted from improvements in education levels, and that female education is far more important than male education for reducing both adult and child mortality.

k. **Displacements**, families sliding into poverty, lack of proper nutrition due to wiped-out crops, lack of access to safe drinking water, **disruption** in schooling, air pollution, disrupted access to healthcare, and prolonged mental stress are all determinants negatively affected by climate change. Their combined and cumulative impact seriously affects human health and contributes heavily to **morbidity** and mortality.

## Reading Passage 2 — Sustainability and Development

Read the passage carefully, then answer the questions and complete the vocabulary exercises below.

---

a. Sustainability is the foundation of long-term progress. It refers to the ability to meet present needs without compromising the future. A sustainable approach ensures that resources are used responsibly, preserving ecosystems and promoting renewability. Without sustainability, development becomes unsustainable, leading to irreversible damage to the planet.

b. Development, on the other hand, is the process of improving the quality of life for individuals and communities. It includes advancements in education, healthcare, infrastructure, and technology. However, overdevelopment — growth without regard for environmental or social consequences — can be harmful. That is why balanced development is essential: it must be both economically viable and environmentally sound.

c. One major challenge is deforestation, the removal of trees at an alarming rate. This leads to soil erosion, loss of biodiversity, and contributes to climate change. To counter this, reforestation and afforestation efforts are being promoted globally. These initiatives aim to restore degraded land and rebuild natural habitats, making the environment more livable and resilient.

d. Another issue is pollution, which affects air, water, and soil. Industrialization and urbanization have led to increased waste and unregulated emissions. Solutions include recycling, waste management, and eco-friendly technologies. By adopting cleaner practices, societies can become more sustainable and pollution-free.

e. Education plays a crucial role in promoting sustainability. Through awareness campaigns and environmental education, individuals become more knowledgeable and motivated to act. Empowerment of youth and communities leads to collective action and meaningful change. A well-informed population is capable of making responsible decisions.

f. Governments and organizations must work collaboratively to create policies that support sustainable development. This includes investment in renewable energy, enforcement of environmental laws, and encouragement of green innovation. Without commitment, these efforts may be ineffective or short-lived. Long-term stability requires consistent and inclusive planning.

g. In conclusion, sustainability and development are interconnected goals. Achieving them requires thoughtfulness, adaptability, and action. A future-oriented mindset, paired with education, can lead to a more equitable, prosperous, and sustainable world.
