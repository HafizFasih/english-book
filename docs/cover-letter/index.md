# Chapter 7 — Cover Letter

A **cover letter** accompanies your résumé when applying for a job. Its purpose is to introduce you to the employer, highlight your most relevant qualifications, and persuade them to read your résumé and invite you for an interview.

:::tip
A generic cover letter is worse than no cover letter. Every cover letter must be **customised** for the specific job and company.
:::

---

## 7.1 Purpose of a Cover Letter

A cover letter:
- Introduces you to the hiring manager
- Explains **why** you are interested in this specific position and company
- Highlights the most relevant qualifications from your résumé
- Shows your communication skills and professional writing ability
- Distinguishes you from other candidates

---

## 7.2 Structure of a Cover Letter

A professional cover letter has four distinct parts:

### Part 1 — Opening Paragraph
State clearly:
- The **position you are applying for**
- **How you found out** about the job (job listing, referral, company website)
- A brief, compelling statement about why you are a strong fit

### Part 2 — Body Paragraph(s)
This is the most important part. Here you:
- Connect your **skills and experience** to the job requirements
- Provide **specific examples** of accomplishments
- Demonstrate that you understand the company's needs
- Do not just repeat your résumé — interpret and expand on it

### Part 3 — Closing Paragraph
- Express enthusiasm for the role
- Request an interview or indicate you will follow up
- Thank the reader for their time and consideration

### Part 4 — Sign-off
Use a formal closing: *Yours sincerely* or *Best regards*, followed by your full name.

---

## 7.3 Cover Letter Format

```
[Your Name]
[Your Address]
[City, Postal Code]
[Your Email] | [Your Phone Number]
[Date]

[Hiring Manager's Name]
[Their Designation]
[Company Name]
[Company Address]

Dear [Mr./Ms./Dr. Last Name],

[Opening paragraph — position applied for, source of job posting, brief hook]

[Body paragraph 1 — your most relevant qualification and specific achievement]

[Body paragraph 2 — why this company/role specifically, what you can contribute]

[Closing paragraph — call to action, request for interview, thank you]

Yours sincerely,
[Your Full Name]
```

---

## 7.4 What to Avoid

| Mistake | Why It Hurts |
|---------|-------------|
| Starting with *"I am writing to apply for…"* | Weak, generic, and overused |
| Repeating the résumé word-for-word | Wastes the reader's time |
| Using vague phrases like *"hard-working" or "team player"* | Means nothing without evidence |
| Addressing it *"To Whom It May Concern"* | Signals you did no research |
| Exceeding one page | Recruiters spend seconds on cover letters |

---

## 7.5 Action Verbs for Cover Letters

Use strong action verbs to describe your experience:

| Category | Verbs |
|----------|-------|
| **Leadership** | led, directed, managed, coordinated, supervised |
| **Achievement** | achieved, exceeded, improved, increased, reduced |
| **Communication** | presented, negotiated, authored, collaborated, liaised |
| **Technical** | designed, developed, implemented, programmed, engineered |
| **Analysis** | analysed, evaluated, assessed, researched, investigated |

## 7.8 Formatting & Style Guidelines

Your cover letter may be one of tens or hundreds the employer receives, so it must be formatted simply, concisely, and be easy to read. It should include:

- One A4 page, typed using a simple 12-point font (e.g., Times New Roman, Arial)
- Plenty of white space (wide margins)
- Spell-checked and grammar-checked
- Well-constructed sentences — not "padded out"
- A positive and straightforward tone
- No slang, SMS abbreviations, or casual terms

---

## 7.9 Content Guidelines

The content should reflect your:
- Written communication skills
- Level of interest in the position
- Knowledge of the organisation
- Relevance of your skills to the selection criteria

**The content should include:**
- Your address and contact details, the recipient's address
- Today's date
- A formal greeting to the relevant contact person
- The purpose of the letter, including the position title and reference number
- Evidence of your interest in the position and the field
- Evidence of your research into the organisation

**The content should NOT include:**
- A simple list of your skills without any supporting evidence of demonstrated skills
- Sentences or phrases copied directly from the organisation's website

## 7.10 Typical Cover Letter Structure (Detailed)

```
Your Address
Suburb / City / Postal Code
Your Telephone / Mobile
Email address
Date

Name of Contact
Title (HR Manager, etc.)
Name of the firm
Address

Dear Mr. / Ms. [Last Name], or Dear Sir/Madam (if name unknown),

OPENING PARAGRAPH — The Fundamentals
What is the purpose of this letter? Introduce yourself and explain why you are writing.
If responding to a job advertisement, include the position title and any reference number.
If canvassing for potential employment, introduce yourself and your current career
circumstances concisely, including any specialised professional interests and abilities.

PARAGRAPHS 2, 3, 4/5 — Why do you want to work for this employer,
and why should they want you?
Cover your current or completed course, planned finishing date, major or specialisation,
and any outstanding academic results. Highlight the skills, expertise, qualities, and
employment experience most relevant to the requirements of the position. Demonstrate
how you meet the selection criteria, and how it relates to both current and future needs
of the employer. Include evidence of key general skills (communication, teamwork,
initiative, self-management) through voluntary or community activities, clubs, etc.

CLOSING PARAGRAPH — What do you want to happen next?
Confirm that you have included your résumé and any other requested documents.
Finish on a positive note, thanking the employer for their time and expressing interest
in attending an interview.

Yours sincerely, (if you addressed them by name)
Yours faithfully, (if you addressed them by title)

[Your Signature]
Your Name
```

## Practice Task

Write a customised cover letter for a job in your engineering or technology field. Your letter should:

1. Be no longer than one page
2. Be addressed to a named hiring manager (you may invent a realistic name)
3. Reference a specific job title and company
4. Include at least one specific achievement or project from your studies
5. Follow the four-part structure described in this chapter
