# Chapter 6 — Letter & Memo Writing

Formal letters and memoranda are essential tools of professional communication. A well-written letter or memo reflects the writer's competence and leaves a lasting impression.

---

## 6.1 Main Parts of a Formal Letter

| Part | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **1. Sender's Address** | Written at the top-left corner. Include house/street number (line 1) and city (line 2). No commas at the end of each line. |
| **2. Date** | Written in full below the sender's address — e.g., *10th January, 20XX* |
| **3. Receiver's Address** | Full name, designation, organisation, and address of the recipient |
| **4. Subject** | The main purpose of the letter in the fewest possible words, yet clear in approach |
| **5. Salutation** | Begin formally: *Dear Sir/Madam* |
| **6. Body** | Three parts: introduction, content/details, and conclusion |
| **7. Complimentary Close** | End formally: *Yours sincerely* or *Yours truly* (capital 'Y') |
| **8. Name & Signature** | Full name (including surname) below the complimentary close |

---

## 6.2 Three Letter Formats

### 1. Block Format

All elements — sender's address, date, salutation, body, and closing — are aligned to the **left margin**. Paragraphs are separated by a double line space. This format has a neat, simple, and professional appearance.

![Block format sample](/img/06-letter-and-memo/Letter%20and%20Memorandum_img002.jpeg)

---

### 2. Modified Block Format

The body paragraphs and recipient's address are **left-justified**. The date, complimentary close, and signature lines begin at the **centre of the page**. Paragraphs may be separated by a single or double line space.

![Modified block format sample](/img/06-letter-and-memo/Letter%20and%20Memorandum_img003.jpeg)

---

### 3. Semi-Block Format

Similar to block format but with a more informal appearance. All elements are left-aligned, but **the first line of each paragraph is indented five spaces**. The date and signature lines are at the centre or slightly right of centre. Paragraphs are separated by a double line space.

![Semi-block format sample](/img/06-letter-and-memo/Letter%20and%20Memorandum_img004.jpeg)

---

## 6.3 Formal Letter Template

```
[Sender's Name]
[Street Address]
[City]

[Date in full]

[Recipient's Name]
[Designation]
[Organisation]
[Address]

Subject: [Brief subject in a few words]

Dear Sir/Madam,

[Introduction — state the reason for writing]

[Body — provide details, explain the situation, or make your request]

[Conclusion — thank the reader, express hope for a response]

Yours sincerely,
[Your Name and Signature]
```

## 6.5 Memorandum (Memo)

A **memorandum** (memo) is a short, internal communication used within an organisation. It is more concise than a letter and uses a standardised format.

### Standard Memo Format

```
MEMORANDUM

TO:      [Recipient's Name(s) / Department]
FROM:    [Sender's Name / Department]
DATE:    [Date]
SUBJECT: [Brief description of the topic]

[Body of the memo — direct and concise]
```

### Key Features of a Memo

- **No salutation** (no "Dear...")
- **No complimentary close** (no "Yours sincerely")
- Uses **bullet points** or numbered lists where appropriate
- Tone is **direct and informative**
- Typically **one page** maximum

### Types of Memos

| Type | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| **Informational** | Share news, updates, or policy changes |
| **Request** | Ask for information, approval, or resources |
| **Confirmation** | Confirm a decision or agreement |
| **Report** | Summarise findings or progress |

### Activity 3 — Block Format Letter Sample

The following is a model letter in block format. Study it carefully before writing your own.

---

**Your Name**
[Your Position / Year of Study]
[Department Name]
[University Name]

[Date]

**Professor [Chairperson's Full Name]**
Chairperson, [Department Name]
[University Name]

**Subject: Request to Arrange Workshop on Digital Tools and Learning Management Systems (LMS)**

Dear Professor [Chairperson's Last Name],

I hope this message finds you well. I am writing to respectfully request your support in arranging a workshop session for undergraduate students on the effective use of digital tools and Learning Management Systems (LMS). As technology continues to play a vital role in higher education, it is essential that students are equipped with the necessary skills to navigate and utilise these platforms efficiently.

Such a workshop would provide students with practical knowledge on accessing course materials, submitting assignments, engaging in online discussions, and making the most of available digital resources. It would also help bridge the gap between traditional learning methods and modern educational practices, ensuring that students remain competitive and adaptable in today's academic environment. By introducing them to these tools, the department can foster greater engagement, improve learning outcomes, and enhance overall academic productivity.

I kindly request your consideration in organising this session at your earliest convenience. Your guidance and support in this initiative will greatly benefit the undergraduate community and strengthen our department's commitment to academic excellence. Thank you for your time and attention to this matter.

Sincerely,

[Your Name]

### Activity 5 — Exemplary Letter Writing Situation

**Situation:** Accidents in big cities are a common phenomenon. Unfortunately, hardly anyone comes to the rescue of road victims because of fear of harassment by the police. Now that the Supreme Court has issued guidelines that people who take road accident victims to a hospital will not be questioned or detained, there is no reason why bystanders should hesitate to help road victims.

**Task:** Write a letter in 100–120 words to the editor of a national daily, requesting him to make people aware of the Supreme Court guidelines and urge them to help people in distress.

**Development tip:** Gather points for the Supreme Court guidelines for helping road victims — taking them to hospital without wasting time, without hesitation — and develop a body paragraph for your letter around these points.
