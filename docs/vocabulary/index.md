# Chapter 2 — Vocabulary Development

A strong vocabulary is built through multiple strategies: understanding words in context, recognising related word forms (word families), identifying words that sound alike but differ in meaning (homophones/homonyms), and knowing opposites (antonyms/synonyms). This chapter covers each of these areas.

---

## 2.1 Inferring Meaning Through Context

When you encounter an unfamiliar word, the surrounding sentences often provide clues to its meaning. Look for:

- **Definition clues** — the word is defined nearby: *"Morbidity, or the rate of disease in a population, has increased."*
- **Synonym clues** — a familiar word with a similar meaning appears nearby.
- **Antonym clues** — a contrast word appears: *"Unlike the insidious slow spread, the acute outbreak was immediately visible."*
- **Example clues** — examples illustrate the meaning: *"Zoonotic diseases — such as rabies and Ebola — spread from animals to humans."*

### Practice

Read each sentence and infer the meaning of the bold word:

1. The scientist used an **intricate** series of instruments; the setup was so complex it took hours to calibrate.
2. The politician gave an **ambiguous** answer — no one could tell whether he supported the policy or opposed it.
3. The charity **alleviated** the suffering of flood victims by providing food, shelter, and medicine.
4. Her **tenacious** grip on the project surprised everyone; she simply refused to give up.

## 2.2 Homophones and Homonyms

### Homophones
**Homophones** are words that *sound the same* but have **different meanings and spellings**.

| Pair | Example Sentences |
|------|-------------------|
| air / heir | The *air* was crisp. The *heir* to the throne arrived. |
| allowed / aloud | Students are *allowed* to leave. Do not read *aloud* in the library. |
| bean / been | She ate a *bean*. Have you *been* to Lahore? |
| break / brake | Take a *break*. Press the *brake* to stop. |
| bare / bear | The *bare* floor was cold. A *bear* crossed the road. |
| buy / by / bye | *Buy* some bread. Stand *by* me. *Bye* for now. |
| flour / flower | Add a cup of *flour*. Pick a *flower* from the garden. |
| hear / here | Can you *hear* me? Come *here* at once. |
| mail / male | Post the *mail*. The *male* student spoke first. |
| peace / piece | We need *peace*. Give me a *piece* of cake. |
| write / right | *Write* your name. Turn *right* at the corner. |

## 2.5 Transition Words (Connectors)

**Transition words** link ideas within and between sentences, helping readers follow the logic of your writing.

![Connectors chart](/img/02-vocabulary/Connectors_img001.jpeg)

| Function | Transition Words |
|----------|-----------------|
| **Adding information** | furthermore, moreover, in addition, besides, also |
| **Showing contrast** | however, nevertheless, on the other hand, although, yet |
| **Showing cause/effect** | therefore, consequently, as a result, hence, thus |
| **Showing sequence** | first, second, then, next, finally, subsequently |
| **Giving examples** | for example, for instance, such as, namely, that is |
| **Summarising** | in conclusion, in summary, to sum up, overall |
| **Emphasising** | indeed, in fact, certainly, clearly, above all |

## 2.6 Word Formation: Compounding, Conversion & Clipping

### Compounding
When two separate words combine to form a single new word:

| Parts | Compound Word |
|-------|--------------|
| book + case | bookcase |
| door + knob | doorknob |
| finger + print | fingerprint |
| sun + burn | sunburn |
| text + book | textbook |
| wall + paper | wallpaper |

### Conversion
A word changes its grammatical function without changing its form.

*Nouns used as verbs:*

| Original Noun | As a Verb |
|---------------|-----------|
| talk | Let's **talk** about this. |
| drink | He will **drink** water. |
| email | Please **email** me the report. |
| dress | She will **dress** formally. |

### Clipping
Longer words are shortened in casual speech:

| Full Word | Clipped Form |
|-----------|-------------|
| facsimile | fax |
| gasoline | gas |
| examination | exam |
| laboratory | lab |
| photograph | photo |
| advertisement | ad |
| gymnasium | gym |

## 2.16 Transition Words — Full Reference and Exercises

![Connectors — sample paragraphs](/img/02-vocabulary/Connectors_img001.jpeg)

### Giving Examples
*For example / For instance / Namely*

"There are two problems: **namely**, the expense and the time."

### Adding Information
*And / In addition / As well as / Also / Too / Furthermore / Moreover / Apart from / Besides*

- **Also** adds an extra idea: "We **also** spoke about marketing."
- **Moreover / Furthermore** add extra information to a point: "Marketing plans give us an idea of the potential market. **Moreover**, they tell us about the competition."
- **Apart from / Besides** mean *as well as*: "**Apart from** Rover, we are the largest sports car manufacturer."

### Summarising
*In short / In brief / In summary / To summarise / In a nutshell / To conclude / In conclusion*

These normally appear at the beginning of a sentence to summarise what has been said.

### Sequencing Ideas
*Firstly, secondly, finally / The former … the latter / The first point is / Lastly / The following*

- **The former … the latter** refer to two previously mentioned things: "Marketing and finance are both covered in the course. **The former** is studied in the first term and **the latter** in the final term."

### Giving a Reason
*Due to / Owing to / Because / Because of / Since / As*

- **Due to / Owing to** must be followed by a noun: "**Due to** the rise in oil prices, the inflation rate rose."
- If followed by a clause, use **the fact that**: "**Due to the fact that** oil prices have risen, the inflation rate went up."
- **Because of** is followed by a noun; **because** can start a sentence or appear in the middle.
- **Since / As** mean *because*: "**Since** the company is expanding, we need to hire more staff."

### Giving a Result
*Therefore / So / Consequently / This means that / As a result*

"The company are expanding. **Therefore / So / Consequently / As a result**, they are taking on extra staff."
(**So** is more informal.)

### Contrasting Ideas
*But / However / Although / Even though / Despite / In spite of / Nevertheless / Nonetheless / While / Whereas / Unlike / In theory … in practice*

- **Although / Despite / In spite of** introduce contrast and need two halves: "**Although** it was cold, she went out in shorts."
- **Despite / In spite of** must be followed by a noun (or *the fact that* + clause): "**Despite the fact that** the company was doing badly, they took on extra employees."
- **Nevertheless / Nonetheless** mean *in spite of that*: "The sea was cold, but he went swimming **nevertheless**."
- **While / Whereas / Unlike** show how two things differ: "**While** my sister has blue eyes, mine are brown."
- **In theory … in practice** show an unexpected result: "**In theory**, teachers should prepare for lessons, but **in practice**, they often don't have enough time."
