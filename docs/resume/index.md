# Chapter 8 — Résumé Writing

A **résumé** is a concise, targeted document that summarises your education, work experience, skills, and achievements for a prospective employer. Your goal is to make your résumé fit the targeted position and stand out — it is an advertisement that sells *you* for a specific job.

---

## 8.1 Types of Résumé

### 1. Chronological Résumé

The most popular format. Lists work history **job by job, starting with the most recent position**.

**Best for:**
- Candidates with relevant experience in their field
- Those who show steady career growth

**Less suitable for:**
- People who have changed jobs frequently
- Those with gaps in their employment record

### 2. Functional Résumé

Focuses on **skills and accomplishments** rather than employment history. Groups skills into categories such as *Management Skills* or *Communication Skills*.

**Best for:**
- Recent graduates with little work experience
- Career changers entering a new field
- Those who want to downplay gaps in employment
- Older job seekers who want to highlight capability over length of history

## 8.2 Résumé Categories

A customised résumé includes some or all of the following sections, arranged strategically:

### 1. Main Heading
Always at the top. Include:
- Your **full name** (formatted prominently)
- Complete **address**
- **Phone number**
- **Email address**

Do not include the word "résumé" as a heading.

### 2. Career Objective
A one- to two-sentence statement tailored to the specific job. Describes the position you seek and what you bring to it.

> *Example: To obtain a civil engineering position at a construction firm where I can apply my expertise in structural analysis and project management to deliver high-quality infrastructure solutions.*

### 3. Summary of Qualifications
Three to eight bulleted statements that prove you are the ideal candidate. Include relevant:
- Experience
- Education highlights
- Unique skills
- Certifications
- Awards and accomplishments

### 4. Education
- Name and location of school(s)
- Dates of attendance
- Major field of study
- Degree received
- GPA (only if strong; omit if low)

> **Note:** In a functional résumé, place education *below* your skills summaries.

### 5. Work Experience / Employment History
List most recent job first. For each position include:
- Employer's name, city
- Dates of employment (month and year)
- Job title
- Significant duties, accomplishments, and promotions — in **bullet point format using action verbs**

> **Tip:** Do not use complete sentences in bullet points. Avoid personal pronouns (I, me, my).

### 6. Capabilities and Skills
- Software and technology proficiencies
- Languages spoken
- Certifications and training
- Communication, writing, or management skills

For a **functional résumé**, this section appears at the top and is grouped into categories:
- *Accounting / Finance Skills*
- *Management / Leadership Skills*
- *Communication / Teamwork Skills*
- *Computer / Technology Skills*

Each category contains bulleted achievements with specific numbers where possible.

### 7. Awards and Activities
Academic honours, professional memberships, volunteer work, and extracurricular activities relevant to the target job.

### 8. Personal Data
Modern résumés generally **omit** birth date, marital status, national origin, and similar personal information.

---

## 8.3 Action Verbs for Your Résumé

Strong résumés use active, specific verbs:

| Category | Verbs |
|----------|-------|
| **Management** | coordinated, directed, managed, led, supervised |
| **Communication** | authored, presented, negotiated, collaborated, corresponded |
| **Achievement** | achieved, improved, exceeded, reduced, increased |
| **Technical** | designed, developed, engineered, implemented, programmed |
| **Analysis** | analysed, evaluated, researched, assessed, investigated |
| **Support** | assisted, facilitated, maintained, organised, processed |

---

## 8.4 Sample Résumé Structure (Functional)

```
FULL NAME
Address | Phone | Email

OBJECTIVE
[One focused sentence about the position and what you offer]

SUMMARY OF QUALIFICATIONS
• [Achievement or qualification 1]
• [Achievement or qualification 2]
• [Achievement or qualification 3]

SKILLS
Technical Skills
• [Skill 1 with brief context]
• [Skill 2 with brief context]

Communication Skills
• [Example of communication achievement]

EDUCATION
[Degree], [Institution], [Year]
GPA: [if strong]

CERTIFICATIONS
• [Certification name], [Issuing body], [Year]

REFERENCES
Available upon request.
```

## Practice Assignment

Create a **functional résumé** based on your own credentials and skills:

1. Use the **functional résumé format** only
2. Organise headings in the sequence taught in this chapter
3. Develop an effective **objective statement** tailored to a job in your field
4. Use **action verbs** throughout the skills sections
5. Keep it to **one page**
6. Assume a realistic job description related to your degree programme and align your objective, skills, and certifications

**Recommended tools:** Canva, Microsoft Word, or LaTeX

---

## 8.6 References

Prepare a separate reference list when you begin your job search. Ask three to five people whether they would be willing to answer inquiries regarding your qualifications:

- Instructors
- Current or previous employers
- Colleagues or subordinates
- Other professional contacts

**Do not include personal references** — companies are more interested in the opinions of objective individuals who know how you perform professionally and academically. Most recruiters see little reason for including the statement *"References furnished upon request"* — it is unnecessary and takes up precious space.
