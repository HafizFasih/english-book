## Q1 — Reading Comprehension *(12 marks)*

### Passage: *How Humans Evolved Language*

Thanks to the field of linguistics we know much about the development of the 5,000 plus languages in existence today. We can describe their grammar and pronunciation and see how their spoken and written forms have changed over time. For example, we understand the origins of the Indo-European group of languages, which includes Norwegian, Hindi and English, and can trace them back to tribes in eastern Europe in about 3000 BC. So, we have mapped out a great deal of the history of language, but there are still areas we know little about. Experts are beginning to look to the field of evolutionary biology to find out how the human species developed to be able to use language. So far, there are far more questions and half-theories than answers.

We know that human language is far more complex than that of even our nearest and most intelligent relatives like chimpanzees. We can express complex thoughts, convey subtle emotions and communicate about abstract concepts such as past and future. And we do this following a set of structural rules, known as grammar. Do only humans use an innate system of rules to govern the order of words? Perhaps not, as some research may suggest dolphins share this capability because they are able to recognise when these rules are broken.

If we want to know where our capability for complex language came from, we need to look at how our brains are different from other animals. This relates to more than just brain size; it is important what other things our brains can do and when and why they evolved that way. And for this there are very few physical clues: artefacts left by our ancestors don't tell us what speech they were capable of making. One thing we can see in the remains of early humans, however, is the development of the mouth, throat and tongue. By about 100,000 years ago, humans had evolved the ability to create complex sounds. Before that, evolutionary biologists can only guess whether or not early humans communicated using more basic sounds.

Another question is, what is it about human brains that allowed language to evolve in a way that it did not in other primates? At some point, our brains became able to make our mouths produce vowel and consonant sounds, and we developed the capacity to invent words to name things around us. These were the basic ingredients for complex language. The next change would have been to put those words into sentences, similar to the 'protolanguage' children use when they first learn to speak. No one knows if the next step – adding grammar to signal past, present and future, for example, or plurals and relative clauses – required a further development in the human brain or was simply a response to our increasingly civilised way of living together.

Between 100,000 and 50,000 years ago, though, we start to see the evidence of early human civilisation, through cave paintings for example; no one knows the connection between this and language. Brains didn't suddenly get bigger, yet humans did become more complex and more intelligent. Was it using language that caused their brains to develop? Or did their more complex brains start producing language? More questions lie in looking at the influence of genetics on brain and language development. Are there genes that mutated and gave us language ability? Researchers have found a gene mutation that occurred between 200,000 and 100,000 years ago, which seems to have a connection with speaking and how our brains control our mouths and face. Monkeys have a similar gene, but it did not undergo this mutation. It's too early to say how much influence genes have on language, but one day the answers might be found in our DNA.

**Title: The Mystery of Human Language Development**

The field of linguistics had provided much knowledge about how languages changed over time, but the question of how humans first developed language remained largely unanswered. Scientists knew that human language was far more complex than that of any other creature, as it involved grammar, abstract thought, and emotional expression. **The Effects of Climate Change**

Across the world, glaciers are melting, sea levels are rising, and extreme weather events are becoming more frequent. These are not predictions about a distant future — they are happening right now. <u>Climate change is one of the most serious threats facing humanity today, and its effects on the environment, human health, and food security are already devastating.</u>

<u>The most visible effect of climate change is the damage it causes to the natural environment.</u> Rising temperatures are melting polar ice caps and glaciers, leading to higher sea levels that threaten coastal cities and islands. Prolonged droughts are turning fertile land into desert, while extreme rainfall causes deadly floods. **Furthermore**, thousands of plant and animal species are losing their habitats and facing extinction as the planet warms.

<u>Climate change also directly threatens human health and food security.</u> Heatwaves are becoming more intense and deadly, especially for the elderly and the poor. Changing rainfall patterns disrupt crop growing seasons, reducing food production and increasing hunger, particularly in developing countries like Pakistan. **In addition**, the spread of diseases like malaria and dengue is increasing as warmer temperatures allow disease-carrying mosquitoes to survive in new regions.

In conclusion, the effects of climate change are wide-ranging and deeply serious. Every government, industry, and individual must take immediate action to reduce carbon emissions, switch to clean energy, and protect our natural environment. <u>The time to act is now — before the damage becomes irreversible.</u>

## Q6 — Report Writing *(12 marks)*

Write a short report on the significance of **internship and professional training** for second to final year students. Discuss opportunities and challenges. Conclude with recommendations.

<details>
<summary>View Answer</summary>

**The Significance of Internship and Professional Training for Engineering Students**

*Submitted by: Computing Society, NED University of Engineering & Technology*
*Date: August 2022*

---

**Introduction**

This report examines the importance of internship and professional training for second to final year engineering students at NED University. It discusses the benefits of practical experience, the current opportunities available, the challenges students face, and recommendations for improvement.

**Significance of Internship and Professional Training**

Internships bridge the gap between academic learning and real-world professional practice. Students who complete internships gain hands-on technical skills, develop professional communication abilities, and build a network of industry contacts. Employers consistently report that graduates with internship experience adapt to the workplace more quickly and perform at a higher level. Furthermore, internships help students discover their interests and confirm whether their chosen career path is the right fit.

**Current Opportunities and Challenges**

Several well-known companies in Karachi — including Engro, Systems Limited, and Habib Bank — offer internship programmes for engineering students during summer. Government initiatives such as the Prime Minister's Youth Programme also provide paid internship placements. However, students face several challenges: a limited number of positions compared to the large student population, unpaid or poorly compensated internships, and a lack of university guidance on how to apply effectively. Many students are also unaware of available opportunities.

**Conclusion and Recommendations**

Internship and professional training are essential components of a well-rounded engineering education. It is recommended that:

- The university establish a dedicated Career Services office to connect students with employers
- Faculty members actively guide students on CV writing and interview preparation
- Compulsory internship credits be included in the final year curriculum
- Companies be invited to campus for recruitment drives twice a year
