## Question 1 — Reading Comprehension *(14 marks)* [CLO-2]

Read the following passage carefully and answer the questions that follow.

### Passage: *The Power of Habits*

Every action you take is shaped by a tiny decision that your brain makes in a fraction of a second. Over time, repeated decisions become automatic — they turn into habits. A habit is a **routine** behaviour that is triggered by a specific cue and followed by a reward. According to James Clear, author of *Atomic Habits*, habits are the compound interest of self-improvement. Small habits, **consistently** practiced, lead to remarkable results over months and years.

Scientists have found that habits are stored in the basal ganglia, a part of the brain that operates largely outside conscious awareness. This explains why habits feel so effortless once they are formed — you no longer have to actively think about them. For example, when you first learned to ride a bicycle, it required intense **concentration** and effort. After months of practice, it became automatic. The same principle applies to study habits, exercise routines, and even negative habits like biting your nails.

Habits follow a three-step loop, known as the Habit Loop: a **cue** that triggers the behaviour, a routine (the behaviour itself), and a reward that reinforces the brain to repeat it. To build a new habit, you must identify the right cue and make the reward immediate and satisfying. To break a bad habit, you must either remove the cue, change the routine that follows it, or **eliminate** the reward.

One of the most powerful insights in habit research is the concept of "keystone habits" — habits that have a **disproportionate** positive effect on other areas of life. Exercise is a classic example. People who start exercising regularly often find that they also begin eating better, sleeping more deeply, and feeling more **productive** at work or in their studies. One good habit can trigger a chain reaction of positive changes across an entire lifestyle.

The most important thing to understand about habits is that they are not destiny. You were not born with bad habits — you **acquired** them through repeated choices. This means you can also unlearn them and replace them with better ones. Change is slow, and progress is not always visible. But with patience and the right strategies, anyone can redesign their habits and ultimately redesign their life.
