## Q1 — Reading Comprehension *(16 marks)*

### Passage: *Science, Morality and Happiness*

Life today is governed by the offshoots of science. We have many new comforts of life that our forefathers did not possess. Should we jump to the conclusion that we are really happier than our forefathers were? It is a very puzzling question. Let us first see what kind of life they led.

The world of our forefathers was different from that of today. They lived in villages. Scientific inventions were not made. There were no planes, cars, movies, radios, etc. T.V, mobile phones, or the internet were not invented. There was no electricity so they were deprived of all those comforts which we now enjoy with the help of electricity. They moved in jolting, slow-moving carts or carriages. Life was simple. They were engaged in tilling the soil for a living. There were many cool and sheltered spots for innocent pleasures. They took active part in all sorts of simple and innocent games. Contentment and happiness prevailed.

In our modern age, life is full of comforts and luxuries. We wonder how our forefathers lived without them. These are the blessings of science. We can travel in railways, airplanes, ships, cars, and motorbikes and many more vehicles like them. The old days of trouble and difficulties have come to an end. In every walk of life we have begun to use electricity. Medical science has done a great service to man. Even the most serious diseases are cured very easily. We have comfortable homes, fine schools, hospitals and many other amenities of life.

The point is we still feel doubt if we are really happier than our forefathers. Material civilization has no doubt made a wonderful stride but the moral development of man has been totally neglected. It has nothing to do with happiness, goodness, beauty, courage, adventure, journey, friendship, love, etc. All these values are necessary for a happy life. Scientific development has out-stripped our spiritual or moral progress.

We are living in an age where we admire richer ones more than those who have spiritual power, and long for luxury of the body than peace of mind. This age of materialism will not be happy till there is an emphasis on the old moral values taught by our forefathers. True happiness is the state of mind that can be better attained in the cottage than in the king's palace.

*(No. of words: 392)*

Write a précis of the passage. Also suggest a suitable title.

**Title: Science, Comfort, and the Loss of Moral Values**

The author questioned whether modern people, despite having more comfort than their forefathers, were truly happier. He noted that their forefathers lived simple village lives without electricity, cars, or technology, yet they enjoyed peace, contentment, and innocent pleasures. In contrast, modern life offered railways, hospitals, electricity, and many other conveniences, all made possible by science. However, the author argued that while material progress had been made, moral and spiritual development had been completely neglected. He warned that people had become overly focused on wealth and luxury, losing important values such as friendship, love, and goodness. He concluded that true happiness was a state of mind that could be better found in a simple life than in a palace.

<details>
<summary>View Answer — Topic 2: People Have Become Overly Dependent on Technology</summary>

**Brainstorming:** Cannot do simple tasks without phone / No memory without GPS / Social relationships reduced / Health affected / Cannot cook without YouTube

---

**People Have Become Overly Dependent on Technology**

Imagine waking up one morning to find that your phone has no charge, the internet is down, and all your apps have stopped working. For most people, this thought alone causes anxiety. We live in a world where technology has become so deeply embedded in our daily lives that many of us have forgotten how to function without it. <u>While technology has made life easier and more convenient, people have become dangerously over-dependent on it, losing basic life skills and the ability to think independently.</u>

<u>One clear sign of this over-dependence is the loss of basic mental abilities.</u> A generation ago, people remembered phone numbers, calculated bills in their heads, and found their way using paper maps. **Today**, most people cannot do any of these things without a smartphone. GPS has replaced our sense of direction. Calculators have replaced mental arithmetic. Search engines have replaced memory. We no longer need to remember anything — we simply "Google it." While this saves time, it also means our brains are becoming lazy and less capable over time.

<u>Another serious problem is that technology has weakened real human connections.</u> People sit in the same room but scroll through their phones instead of talking. Families eat meals in silence while watching screens. Children prefer video games over outdoor play and face-to-face friendships. **As a result**, loneliness and social anxiety have increased significantly among young people, even though they are more "connected" online than ever before.

<u>Furthermore, over-dependence on technology has serious health consequences.</u> Sitting in front of screens for hours every day leads to poor posture, eye strain, sleep problems, and physical inactivity. The rise in obesity, especially among young people, is directly linked to a sedentary lifestyle driven by excessive screen time.

In conclusion, technology is a powerful tool, but we have allowed it to become our master rather than our servant. We must make a conscious effort to limit screen time, rebuild our basic skills, and invest in real relationships. <u>Technology should serve human life, not replace it.</u>

## Q5 — Report Writing *(12 marks)*

You visited **Book Exhibition 2023** at Expo Centre. Write an informational report of **250–300 words** to submit to your English teacher.

**Structure:** Title / By / Introduction / Details / Conclusion

<details>
<summary>View Answer</summary>

**Book Exhibition 2023 — A Celebration of Knowledge and Reading**

*By: Ahmed Ali, First Year, CS Department, NED University*

---

**Introduction**

The Book Exhibition 2023 was held at the Expo Centre, Karachi, from 1st to 5th August 2023. The event was organized by the Pakistan Publishers and Booksellers Association and was open to the public free of charge. The exhibition attracted thousands of visitors over its five-day run, including students, teachers, writers, and general book lovers. Its main purpose was to promote a reading culture in Pakistan and to connect publishers with readers.

**Details**

The exhibition featured over 200 stalls from local and international publishers. More than 50,000 books were on display, covering a wide range of genres including science and technology, fiction, Islamic studies, self-help, children's literature, and academic textbooks. Books were available in both Urdu and English. A large number of stalls offered discounts of 20 to 40 percent, making books affordable for students and families. The public response was outstanding — long queues were seen at popular stalls throughout the day. Several authors were also present and held book signing sessions, which attracted great interest from young readers.

**Conclusion**

The Book Exhibition 2023 was a highly successful and well-organized event. It provided an excellent opportunity for students and the general public to explore a wide variety of books at affordable prices. The event not only encouraged reading but also helped people discover new authors and genres. It is strongly recommended that such exhibitions be held more frequently and in other cities as well, so that the culture of reading can be promoted across the country.
