## Question 1 — Reading Comprehension *(14 marks)*

Read the passage carefully and answer the questions that follow.

### Passage: *The Diderot Effect*

The French philosopher Denis Diderot lived nearly his entire life in poverty, but that all changed one day in 1765. Diderot's daughter was about to be married and he could not afford to pay for the wedding. Despite his lack of wealth, Diderot was well known for his role as the co-founder and writer of *Encyclopédie*, one of the most **comprehensive** encyclopedias of the time. When Catherine the Great, the Empress of Russia, heard of Diderot's financial troubles, her heart went out to him. She was a book lover and greatly enjoyed his encyclopedia. She offered to buy Diderot's personal library for £1,000—more than $150,000 today. Suddenly, Diderot had money to spare. With his new wealth, he not only paid for the wedding but also **acquired** a scarlet robe for himself.

Diderot's scarlet robe was beautiful. So beautiful, in fact, that he immediately noticed how out of place it seemed when surrounded by his more common possessions. He wrote that there was "no more coordination, no more unity, no more beauty" between his elegant robe and the rest of his stuff.

Diderot soon felt the urge to upgrade his possessions. He replaced his rug with one from Damascus. He decorated his home with expensive sculptures. He bought a mirror to place above the mantel, and a better kitchen table. He tossed aside his old straw chair for a leather one. Like falling dominoes, one purchase led to the next. Diderot's behaviour is not uncommon. In fact, the **tendency** for one purchase to lead to another one has a name: the Diderot Effect. The Diderot Effect states that obtaining a new possession often creates a spiral of **consumption** that leads to additional purchases.

You can spot this pattern everywhere. You buy a dress and have to get new shoes and earrings to match. You buy a couch and suddenly question the layout of your entire living room. You buy a toy for your child and soon find yourself purchasing all of the accessories that go with it. It's a chain reaction of purchases. Many human behaviours follow this cycle. You often decide what to do next based on what you have just finished doing. Going to the bathroom leads to washing and drying your hands, which reminds you that you need to put the dirty towels in the laundry, so you add laundry detergent to the shopping list, and so on. No behaviour happens in **isolation**. Each action becomes a cue that triggers the next behaviour.

Why is this important? When it comes to building new habits, you can use the connectedness of behaviour to your advantage. One of the best ways to build a new habit is to identify a current habit you already do each day and then stack your new behaviour on top. This is called habit stacking. *(471 words)*

### Q1.3 — What is "habit stacking"? *(3 marks)*

<details>
<summary>View Answer</summary>

Habit stacking is a technique for building a new habit by attaching it to an existing daily habit. Instead of trying to start a completely new routine from nothing, you identify something you already do every day — such as making tea, brushing your teeth, or sitting at your desk — and add your new habit immediately before or after it. The old habit acts as a natural reminder, making it much easier to remember and repeat the new behavior.

</details>

---

### Q1.4 — How might habit stacking help someone build new habits? *(3 marks)*

<details>
<summary>View Answer</summary>

Habit stacking helps because it removes the difficulty of remembering a new habit. Since the old habit is already automatic, it naturally triggers the new one. For example, if someone wants to start reading more, they can stack it onto an existing habit like drinking morning tea — "After I make my tea, I will read for ten minutes." Over time, this connection becomes strong, and the new habit becomes as automatic as the old one. It also reduces willpower needed, making long-term change more achievable.

</details>

Denis Diderot, a French philosopher, lived in poverty most of his life. When the Empress of Russia bought his library for a large sum, he used the money to buy a beautiful scarlet robe. The robe made all his other belongings look plain and old, so he began replacing them one by one with expensive items. The same principle of connected behavior applies to daily actions — each action becomes a trigger for the next. **Impact of Technology on Education**

From the moment a student wakes up to the time they go to sleep, technology is a constant presence in their lives. It is no surprise, then, that technology has deeply changed the way students learn and teachers teach. <u>Technology has greatly improved education by making it more accessible and engaging, but it also brings challenges such as distraction and inequality that must be carefully managed.</u>

<u>One of the greatest benefits</u> of technology in education is that it removes barriers to learning. Students in remote villages can now attend online classes and access high-quality educational content from the internet. Platforms like Khan Academy, YouTube, and Coursera offer free lessons on almost every subject. This means that a student who cannot afford expensive textbooks can still learn using free digital resources. Furthermore, technology makes learning more interactive through videos, simulations, and instant feedback, which helps students understand difficult concepts more easily.

<u>However</u>, technology in education also has serious downsides. The most common problem is distraction — many students use their laptops and phones for social media, games, or entertainment instead of studying. This reduces their focus and hurts their academic performance. In addition, not every student has access to a computer or a fast internet connection. This creates a digital divide between students from wealthy families and those from poorer backgrounds, which can increase inequality in education.

In conclusion, technology has the power to make education more effective, flexible, and widely available. It opens doors for millions of students around the world. Nevertheless, it is important that schools set clear rules about technology use in classrooms and that governments work to provide equal internet access for all students. With proper guidance and equal access, technology can truly become a powerful tool for a better future.
