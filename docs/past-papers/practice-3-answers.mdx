### Q1.2 — What is the Habit Loop? *(3 marks)*

According to the passage, the Habit Loop is a three-step cycle that explains how habits work:

1. **Cue** — A trigger or signal that starts the behaviour. For example, feeling bored might trigger you to pick up your phone.
2. **Routine** — The actual behaviour that follows the cue. For example, scrolling through social media.
3. **Reward** — The positive feeling or result that reinforces the habit and makes the brain want to repeat it. For example, feeling entertained or distracted from stress.

To build a new habit, you attach it to the right cue and make its reward immediate and satisfying.

---

### Q1.3 — What are Keystone Habits? *(3 marks)*

Keystone habits are habits that have a disproportionately large positive effect on other areas of life, beyond just the area they directly relate to. The passage gives **exercise** as a classic example. When people begin exercising regularly, they often find that they also start eating better, sleeping more deeply, and feeling more productive at work or in their studies. This happens because one good habit triggers a chain reaction of positive changes throughout a person's entire lifestyle. Keystone habits are valuable because they make other positive changes much easier to achieve.

---

### Q1.4 — "Habits are not destiny" *(3 marks)*

When the author says habits "are not destiny," they mean that you are not permanently defined by your current habits. Bad habits are not a part of your personality that you were born with — they were acquired through repeated choices made over time. Since habits were learned, they can also be unlearned. The author supports this by explaining that change is possible with "patience and the right strategies," and that anyone can replace bad habits with better ones by deliberately redesigning their routine.

## Question 2 — Précis Writing *(10 marks)*

**Title: How Habits Are Formed and How to Change Them**

According to research, habits were routine behaviours stored in the basal ganglia of the brain that operated automatically once formed. They followed a three-step cycle known as the Habit Loop: a cue, a routine, and a reward. To build a new habit, a person had to identify the right cue and make the reward immediate. To break a bad habit, they had to remove the cue, change the routine, or eliminate the reward. Scientists had also identified "keystone habits" — positive habits like exercise that triggered wider lifestyle improvements. The author concluded that habits were not destiny; since they had been acquired through repeated choices, they could be unlearned and replaced with better ones through patience and deliberate effort.

## Question 4 — Essay Writing *(12 marks)*

**Topic chosen: Engineering students should learn communication skills, not just technical skills.**

**Brainstorming:** Engineers work in teams / Present reports / Write proposals / Client-facing roles / Soft skills gap in Pakistan / Better career outcomes

---

**Engineering Students Should Learn Communication Skills, Not Just Technical Skills**

Ask any employer in Pakistan or abroad what they look for in a fresh engineering graduate, and the answer is almost always the same: technical competence is expected — but communication skills are what set a candidate apart. Yet most engineering programmes in Pakistan spend 90 percent of their time on mathematics, coding, and laboratory work, leaving little room for developing the ability to write clearly, speak confidently, or present ideas effectively. <u>Engineering students must be taught communication skills alongside their technical subjects, because the ability to communicate is just as important as the ability to calculate.</u>

<u>The first reason is that engineers rarely work alone.</u> In the real world, engineers work in teams, coordinate with clients, write technical reports, and present their findings to management. An engineer who cannot explain their work clearly is a liability, no matter how brilliant their calculations are. <u>Furthermore</u>, many engineers move into leadership roles as they advance in their careers — and leadership is almost entirely about communication: motivating teams, managing conflicts, and making decisions collaboratively.

<u>Secondly, strong communication skills directly improve career outcomes.</u> Research consistently shows that graduates with good writing and presentation skills earn higher starting salaries and get promoted faster than those with technical skills alone. In an increasingly competitive job market, communication skills are often the deciding factor in who gets hired and who does not.

In conclusion, engineering is not just about solving technical problems — it is about communicating those solutions to others effectively. <u>Universities that integrate communication training into engineering curricula are not diluting technical education; they are completing it.</u>
