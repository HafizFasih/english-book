---

### 1(b) Short Answers *(6 marks)*

**9.** The author says the problem is passive consumption, not social media itself, because the platform is not harmful on its own — it is how people use it that matters. The passage provides evidence from the University of Pennsylvania study, which showed that simply reducing use to 30 minutes a day led to major improvements in mood and loneliness. The research found that endlessly scrolling and comparing yourself to others causes harm, while actively chatting and engaging does not.

**10.** A student who uses social media passively for more than three hours every night before sleeping would likely suffer from poor sleep quality, because blue light from the screen reduces melatonin production. Poor sleep would then lead to increased anxiety, weak concentration in lectures, and reduced ability to cope with academic stress — all of which would negatively affect their academic performance and overall well-being.

**11.** Active use means engaging with social media purposefully — chatting with real friends, creating content, or participating in helpful communities. Passive use means silently watching other people's lives, scrolling through filtered images of other people's achievements and appearances, and comparing yourself to them without interacting. The passage suggests active use can be beneficial, while passive use causes emotional harm.

**12.** The author recommends curating your feed to solve the problem of toxic comparison. If a person's feed is filled with unrealistic, filtered images of perfect lives, it will cause envy, low self-esteem, and dissatisfaction. By selecting only content that is uplifting, educational, or genuinely helpful, a person can use social media without being harmed by it. Curating the feed puts control back in the hands of the user.

## Q3 — Summary Writing *(5 marks)*

Social media has raised serious concerns about its effects on young people's mental health. The main concern is that passive use — endlessly scrolling and comparing yourself to others — leads to feelings of loneliness and depression. Two key negative effects are poor sleep caused by blue light from screens, and increased anxiety linked to social comparison. However, social media can be beneficial when used actively and intentionally, particularly for young people seeking social support through online communities.

## Q4 — Essay Writing *(10 marks)*

**Topic chosen: Pakistan's youth have the potential to become global leaders in technology.**

**Pakistan's Youth: The Future of Global Technology**

Pakistan is a country where 64 percent of the population is under the age of 30. This is not just a statistic — it is a competitive advantage. In a world being reshaped by artificial intelligence, software, and digital innovation, a young, energetic, and increasingly educated workforce is exactly what is needed. <u>Pakistan's youth have the real potential to become global leaders in technology, provided that the right investment is made in education, infrastructure, and entrepreneurship.</u>

<u>First, Pakistani youth have already proven their talent on the global stage.</u> Pakistan consistently ranks among the top countries in the world for freelancing income, with thousands of young developers, designers, and digital marketers earning in foreign currency from home. Platforms like Upwork and Fiverr have allowed talented young Pakistanis to compete directly with professionals from the United States and Europe — and win. <u>Furthermore</u>, tech startups founded by young Pakistanis are attracting international investment, proving that the talent and ambition needed to compete globally already exist here.

<u>However</u>, realising this potential on a national scale requires serious, sustained investment. Access to quality tech education, reliable internet connectivity, affordable laptops, and English language training must be made available to all young Pakistanis — not just those in elite schools in major cities. The government and private sector must partner to create incubators, coding boot camps, and scholarship programmes that can scale this talent into a national movement.

In conclusion, Pakistan's youth are not a burden — they are the country's greatest asset. <u>With the right support, they have every capacity to lead the world in technology, innovation, and digital enterprise.</u>

## Q6 — Report Writing *(10 marks)*

**Tech Fest 2025 — Inspiring the Next Generation of Engineers**

*By: Cultural & Technical Committee, Department of Computer Science, NED University*

---

**Introduction**

Tech Fest 2025 was a two-day event organised by the Department of Computer Science at NED University of Engineering & Technology on 20th and 21st November 2025. The event was held at the Main Auditorium and Engineering Block and attracted over 600 participants, including students from various departments, faculty members, and industry guests. The primary goal of the event was to encourage innovation, showcase student talent, and provide a platform for peer learning and industry interaction.

**Main Activities**

The event featured a wide range of competitions and activities. The Robotics Competition saw ten teams compete in building autonomous robots capable of completing an obstacle course. The 24-Hour Coding Challenge had over 80 participants working in teams to develop working software applications under time pressure. A Project Display Area showcased more than 30 final-year and first-year projects to faculty judges and industry visitors. Three guest speakers from leading technology companies delivered inspiring talks on topics including artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, and career development for engineers. Student participation was enthusiastic throughout both days, with several teams winning cash prizes and certificates.

**Conclusion**

Tech Fest 2025 was a highly successful event that motivated students to develop practical skills beyond the classroom. It provided valuable exposure to real-world technology problems and created meaningful connections between students and industry professionals. For next year, it is recommended that the event be extended to three days, that more companies be invited to set up recruitment stalls, and that a dedicated session for first-year students be included to help them understand career pathways from an early stage.
