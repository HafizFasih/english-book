## Q2 *(Compulsory)* — Reading Comprehension

### Passage: *Running Your Own Business*

The pressures of being self-employed are inescapable. You may have to work long hours, and there will be times when things get on top of you. You may well get into debt in order to finance the enterprise. You will need to maintain your faith in your business, often in the face of other people's doubts.

There will be times when you will feel lonely and frustrated. If you employ people, you will need to be positive and show leadership at all times. There will be times when you need to be tough and be prepared to discipline difficult employees or make difficult demands of your suppliers. You need to be polite and helpful, even when an awkward customer is giving you a hard time.

Many of those who successfully start their own business have the backing of their family. Working long hours may have adverse impacts on your family life and your family must be prepared for it. Also, you must be sure that your family can accommodate the risks that self-employment can bring, especially in terms of lower income in the initial stages, and the serious implications if the business fails. You must take the time to talk to all members of your family who might be affected by your decision to be your own boss.

You need to ask yourself several questions before you intend to start your own business venture. Do you have the financial resources, and can you afford to risk them? For example, you might take a secured loan based on the security of your home; what are your plans if the business fails and you are forced to sell your house? Do you have sufficient experience and technical skills to perform the core functions of your new business? Are you knowledgeable enough about the market conditions to be able to assess its needs and adapt to its changes? Do you have the tenacity and discipline to see through hard times when cash inflows will be short and demands will be heavy from customers, bankers, staff, and, crucially, your family?

### Q2.A — Answer the following questions *(6 marks)*

- **(i)** What types of pressures are generally faced by individuals who choose to be self-employed?
- **(ii)** What are the qualities which are vital for the success of self-employed persons?
- **(iii)** List **four** types of queries you must ask yourself before undertaking a self-employed business.

<details>
<summary>View Answers</summary>

**(i)** Self-employed individuals face many pressures. They may have to work long hours, go into debt to finance their business, face loneliness and frustration, manage difficult employees or suppliers, and deal with demanding customers. They must also manage the impact of long working hours on their family life.

**(ii)** The qualities vital for success include: maintaining faith in the business, showing strong leadership, being positive and tough when needed, being polite and patient with customers, having tenacity and discipline to survive difficult financial periods, and having the support and understanding of family.

**(iii)** Four important questions to ask yourself before starting a business:
1. Do you have the financial resources, and can you afford to risk them?
2. Do you have sufficient experience and technical skills to run the business?
3. Are you knowledgeable enough about the market and its changing needs?
4. Do you have the tenacity and discipline to survive periods of low income and high pressure?

## Q4 — Essay Writing *(12 marks)*

Write an essay of **300–350 words** on ANY ONE topic. Underline the **thesis statement** and at least **two transitions**.

1. Impact of technology on education
2. Mental health is as important as physical health.
3. Social media effects on consumer behaviour
4. Humans are causing accelerated climate change.

<details>
<summary>View Answer — Topic: Social Media Effects on Consumer Behaviour</summary>

**Brainstorming:** Influencers promote products / Impulsive buying / FOMO / Targeted ads / Overspending / Materialism

---

**Social Media Effects on Consumer Behaviour**

Scroll through Instagram for just five minutes and you will be shown products, sponsored posts, and influencer recommendations designed to make you spend money. This is not a coincidence — it is a carefully planned system. <u>Social media has dramatically changed consumer behaviour by creating impulsive buying habits, promoting materialism, and making people spend money on things they do not truly need.</u>

<u>One of the biggest effects of social media on consumer behaviour is impulsive buying.</u> In the past, people would research a product, compare prices, and think carefully before purchasing. **Today**, a single post by a popular influencer can make thousands of people buy a product within hours, without stopping to think whether they actually need it. Social media platforms use algorithms to show users exactly what they are most likely to buy, making resistance even harder.

<u>Furthermore, social media promotes a culture of materialism.</u> When people constantly see others displaying new clothes, gadgets, and luxury items, they feel pressure to match that lifestyle. This leads to a phenomenon known as FOMO — the Fear of Missing Out. People buy things not because they need them, but because they want to appear successful and relevant on social media. **As a result**, many young people go into debt trying to maintain a lifestyle they cannot afford.

In conclusion, social media has become one of the most powerful forces shaping how and why people spend money. While it has made shopping more convenient and accessible, it has also made people less thoughtful and more impulsive in their spending habits. <u>Consumers must develop digital literacy and self-awareness to resist the manipulative power of social media and make smarter financial decisions.</u>

*(Word count: 290)*

</details>
