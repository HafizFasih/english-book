## 12.1 Listening Skills — Types of Listening

Before we practise grammar, we cover one important communication skill: **listening**.

Effective communication requires not just speaking and writing, but also purposeful listening. There are several types of listening:

| Type | Description | Example Context |
|------|-------------|----------------|
| **Informational** | Listening to learn facts or data | Attending a lecture on AI |
| **Critical** | Evaluating and analysing what is heard | Listening to a policy debate |
| **Empathetic** | Listening to understand emotions and provide support | Therapy session, personal conversation |
| **Appreciative** | Listening for enjoyment or aesthetic pleasure | Music, storytelling |
| **Discriminative** | Identifying differences in sounds or tone | Identifying sarcasm or stress |

## 12.4 Beneficial Academic Expressions

Use these expressions in essays, presentations, and discussions to sound more professional:

**Introducing Ideas**
- It is widely recognised that…
- According to recent research…
- There is substantial evidence to suggest that…

**Explaining or Giving Examples**
- For instance…
- This can be seen in…
- To illustrate this point…

**Comparing & Contrasting**
- In comparison…
- On the other hand…
- Conversely…
- Similarly…

**Showing Cause & Effect**
- As a result…
- Consequently…
- This leads to…
- Therefore…

**Clarifying & Emphasising**
- It is important to note that…
- Significantly…
- What is particularly striking is…

**Adding Information**
- Furthermore…
- In addition…
- Moreover…

**Concluding or Summarising**
- In conclusion…
- To summarise…
- The evidence clearly indicates that…
- Overall, it can be argued that…

## 12.8 Grammar Reference — Common Tense Errors

### Subject-Verb Agreement

The verb must agree with its subject in number (singular or plural).

| Rule | Correct | Incorrect |
|------|---------|-----------|
| Singular subject → singular verb | *The student **is** ready.* | *The student **are** ready.* |
| Plural subject → plural verb | *The students **are** ready.* | *The students **is** ready.* |
| Collective noun → singular | *The committee **has** decided.* | *The committee **have** decided.* |
| "Neither/either" → singular | *Neither of them **was** present.* | *Neither of them **were** present.* |

### Conditional Sentences

| Type | Structure | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| Zero conditional | *If + present, present* | *If water reaches 100°C, it boils.* |
| First conditional | *If + present, will + base* | *If you study, you will pass.* |
| Second conditional | *If + past, would + base* | *If I knew the answer, I would speak up.* |
| Third conditional | *If + past perfect, would have + past participle* | *If they had studied, they would have passed.* |

### Subjunctive Mood

Used after verbs of suggestion, recommendation, demand, or insistence:

> *He suggested that she **go** (not *goes*) to the library.*
> *The manager insisted that the report **be submitted** (not *is submitted*) immediately.*

## 12.12 Reading and Study Skills — Space Exploration

Read the passage and complete the activities that follow.

### Text: The History of Space Exploration

Space exploration is one of the greatest achievements of the modern age. It began during the Cold War era, when the United States and the Soviet Union competed in the Space Race.

In 1957, the Soviet Union launched Sputnik 1, the first artificial satellite to orbit the Earth. This event marked the beginning of space exploration. Just a few years later, in 1961, Soviet astronaut Yuri Gagarin became the first human in space. In response, the United States accelerated its space programme.

The most famous achievement came in 1969, when American astronaut Neil Armstrong became the first person to walk on the Moon during the Apollo 11 mission. This event was broadcast worldwide and remains a milestone in human history.

Exploration continued through the late 20th century. In 1971, the Soviet Union launched Salyut 1, the first space station, followed later by the U.S. Skylab (1973). Cooperation replaced rivalry with the launch of the International Space Station (ISS) in 1998, involving multiple countries including the U.S., Russia, Japan, and members of the European Space Agency.

In terms of technology, space shuttles like Columbia (first flight in 1981) enabled reusable missions, while robotic explorers such as the Mars Rover Sojourner (1997) expanded human knowledge of other planets. More recently, private companies such as SpaceX have made breakthroughs in reusable rockets, with the first successful landing of a Falcon 9 booster in 2015.

Overall, space exploration has moved from competition to cooperation and now towards commercialisation, opening new possibilities for the future of humanity beyond Earth.
